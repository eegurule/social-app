Circle Planner

The rules of a small, invite-only group for planning real-world get-togethers.
Effective: October 13, 2026 Scale: capped at 25 households Money: none changes hands — this is a community trust experiment, not a commercial product
What you're agreeing to. Circle Planner is not an open platform or a paid service — it's a private group you were personally invited into by someone who knows you. These terms describe how membership, messaging, and events actually work. For how your data is handled specifically, see the companion Privacy Policy.

01Who can join

  • Invite-only, through real-life connections. You can only join because an existing member who knows you in person vouched for you. The join page asks you to name the member who invited you, and sign-ups that can't be matched to an inviting member are not admitted to the group.
  • One household per member, and one outstanding invite per member at a time — you can't queue up several friends against one open slot.
  • Anyone under 18 is a household's child, not a member in their own right. An adult household member is responsible for any minors in the household, and every rule about children in this document and the Privacy Policy applies to them.
  • The group is capped at 25 households. Growth beyond that is deliberately throttled — including by a rule limiting how much of the group's membership can trace back to a single inviter — so no one's ability to join is guaranteed even with a willing vouch.

02How it actually works

There is no group chat. You talk to Claude, an AI assistant, privately and 1:1 — about your availability, what you're up for, who you already know and trust. Claude uses those private conversations to put together real-world event proposals: who's invited, where, and when.

  • Nothing is guaranteed to happen. An idea for an event — yours or someone else's — is evaluated, not promised. "Nothing viable this week" is a normal, expected outcome.
  • A "sure!" is not a commitment. Claude will ask for actual logistics — what time you're arriving, what you're bringing — before counting you as confirmed. Vague or noncommittal answers don't lock in a spot.
  • If the core group for an event falls through, the event is cancelled outright rather than backfilled with whoever's available. If you're notified of a cancellation, that's final for that specific plan.
  • You can propose your own event ideas at any time ("I'm having a birthday party," "I need help moving") — these get evaluated the same way as anything else, not auto-approved.

03No money, no liability transfer

  • Circle Planner does not process payments and is not involved in any money that changes hands between members directly (e.g., splitting the cost of a group activity) — that's between you and the other members.
  • You assume the ordinary risks of in-person group activities you choose to attend, the same as you would attending a friend's gathering. Circle Planner helps coordinate the plan; it does not supervise the event, provide insurance, or guarantee anyone's conduct.
  • A full liability waiver is presented and must be agreed to separately, before you're onboarded — this section is a summary, not a substitute for it.

04Children, supervision, and screening

Ordinary offers

Most things members offer each other (hosting, cooking, a ride to a group event as one of several adults present) require no special screening.

Supervision offers

Anything involving you alone supervising someone else's child — driving them solo, watching them without another parent present — requires a background check before it's treated as active. An unscreened offer is marked pending and never used until cleared.

Before any child is actually dropped off with another household for an event, the receiving parent is shown a full safety-relevant disclosure about the supervising adults and must actively confirm reading it. This is described in full in the Privacy Policy and requires a separate signed acknowledgment for households with children.

05Trust, conduct, and what happens if it breaks down

  • Members can tell Claude, privately, when their trust in someone else has changed. Vague discomfort is handled quietly and never used to remove anyone. Anything specific, or anything involving a child, is escalated to a trained participant — a peer within the group with responsibility for handling exactly this — never posted to the group or handled as casual gossip.
  • Low engagement, not low popularity, is what leads to rotation. Roughly every four weeks, the least-engaged member may be rotated out to make room for others — this is about whether you're responding and participating, not how well-liked you are. Being rotated out is explicitly not a punishment: your history is kept, and you can be re-invited later with everything intact.
  • Removal for cause (as opposed to routine rotation) is reserved for the trust/safety escalation process above, handled by trained participants — not an automated or silent process.
  • You may leave the group at any time, for any reason, by telling Claude or the group's organizer.
